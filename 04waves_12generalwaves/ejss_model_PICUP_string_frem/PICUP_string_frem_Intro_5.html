Part 3: Testing

Once you have your numerical solver coded, you will need to supply initial conditions. At first, consider a stretched but unexcited string, where the two endpoints are held fixed at some distance L′>LL′>L such that the spring bears a tension TT.

  • In terms of the string parameters LL and γγ, and the tension TT, what is L′L′? (That is, how far must you stretch your string to achieve the desired tension?)

  • In terms of L′L′ and your number of segments NN, what initial conditions for the xixi’s and yiyi’s correspond to this stretched but unexcited string?

  • Code these initial conditions and verify that your string doesn’t move. If you haven’t already, make your program animate your vibrating string using your favorite visualization tool.

  • Now, excite your string by displacing it in some fashion and verify qualitatively that it moves realistically. Note that if you choose the simplest thing and displace only one point mass, this corresponds to “whacking a guitar string with a knife”; you will get rather violent oscillations in this case.

  • Modify your program to track conservation of energy. The kinetic energy is just the sum of 12mv212mv2 for all the masses, and the potential energy is the sum of 12k(r−r0)212k(r−r0)2 for all the springs. Verify that your simulation approximately conserves energy.
