Escape Speed


Is it true that “what goes up must come down”?  
It is only accurate to say that “what goes up may come down”.  There is a critical speed at which an object can be launched such that it can escape the Earth permanently.  Such a critical speed is termed as the escape speed.

Example


Determine an expression for the escape speed, v, of a rocket of mass m launched from the surface of Earth of mass M and radius R.

By conservation of energy, assuming at r = ∞, the minimum speed is 0.

KEearth surface + PEearth surface = KE∞ + PE∞

1 2 m v 2 + ( - G m M r e a r t h ) = 0 + 0 

1 2 v 2 + ( - ( 6.67 x 1 0 -11 ) ( 6.0 x 1 0 24 ) 6.37 x 1 0 6 ) = 0 + 0 

v = 1.12x104 m/s

Therefore, to escape from Earth, it implies that the projectile must have a minimum velocity of v = 1.12x104 m/s .
