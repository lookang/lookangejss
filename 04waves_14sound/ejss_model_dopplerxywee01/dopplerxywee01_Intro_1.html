Doppler Effect in 1D

The red source emits wavefronts at a constant period T. The green observer receives them at a different rate when the source, the observer, or both move relative to the medium.

  • Start with a preset, then compare the space view and the timing graph.
  • In the space view, look for compressed wavefronts in front of the source and stretched wavefronts behind it.
  • In the timing graph, red bars mark emission times and green bars mark detection times.
  • If the green bars are closer together than the red bars, the detected frequency f' is higher than the emitted frequency f. If they are farther apart, f' is lower.
  • Use Step to pause and reason about one moment at a time. Drag the source or observer only while paused.
  • The sonic-boom presets are extension cases. In that regime, compare the shock-wave envelope instead of the usual subsonic Doppler formula.

Suggested inquiry: Which changes the received frequency more clearly in this model, moving the source or moving the observer by the same fraction of the sound speed?
